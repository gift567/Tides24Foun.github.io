SGBV Sensitization for School Learners in Zambia

In December 2025, Tides24 Foundation Zambia (TFZ), in collaboration with George Health Facility, conducted Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) sensitization sessions at Diana Kaimba Primary School and George Central School. The sessions equipped learners with age-appropriate knowledge to recognize, prevent, and report SGBV, including understanding safe and unsafe touch and different forms of abuse. Learners also received information on sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR), HIV prevention, counselling, HIV testing, pregnancy screening, and post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP). The initiative strengthened links between schools, health facilities, and community support systems while promoting child protection, safer learning environments, and early help-seeking among children and adolescents. Through school-based SGBV prevention and SRHR education, TFZ continues to support children and adolescents to stay safe, informed, and healthy.

Sis to Sis for Self Care Model: Supporting Young Mothers in Zambia

In 2025, Tides24 Foundation Zambia (TFZ), with support from UNAIDS, introduced the Sis to Sis for Self Care Model to support pregnant and breastfeeding adolescent girls and young women living with or at risk of HIV in Lusaka.

Initially implemented at George, Kanyama West, Chainda and Ng’ombe health facilities, the programme uses trained peer mentors, buddy systems and safe spaces to help young mothers overcome challenges such as stigma, poverty and limited social support. The initiative provides support in HIV prevention and treatment, sexual and reproductive health, mental health, self-care and social empowerment, while connecting young women to healthcare and livelihood opportunities. Through peer support and community connections, Sis to Sis empowers young mothers to take charge of their health and build brighter futures.

Together, we can ensure every young mother has the support, knowledge and opportunity to thrive. Join us in creating healthier, stronger and more empowered communities.
